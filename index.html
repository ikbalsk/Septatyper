Read ciprofloxacin resistance from five genes.

Upload a bacterial genome, or enter a BV-BRC genome ID. Ciprotyper checks the species, reads five codon positions in gyrA, parC and recN and the plasmid genes qnrB2 and qnrS1, and types the MLST sequence type. The seven values form the ciprotype that predicts the phenotype.

How a call is made

  1. Check the speciesThe genome is compared with the species you chose. For a whole genome the MLST genes must match at 90% identity or more, and gyrA, parC and recN must too. If they do not, you get no result, and no sequence type.
  2. Read the genesBLAST finds gyrA, parC and recN and reads the codons at GyrA-83, GyrA-87, ParC-80, ParC-84 and RecN-346. qnrB2 and qnrS1 count as present at 80% identity and 90% coverage.
  3. Type and build the ciprotypeThe seven-locus MLST sequence type is read. The seven values join into the ciprotype, for example L-N-I-E-L-0-0.
  4. Call the phenotypeIf reference isolates of that species and source carry the same ciprotype, their majority phenotype is the call. A new ciprotype is called by three checkpoints: the residue table, RecN-346 and the qnr genes. It is resistant if any checkpoint that applies says resistant.
  5. See whyThe result shows the sequence type, the reference isolates behind the call and the reasoning for each checkpoint.

Reference performance

Accuracy of the majority rule on the founding isolates. The rules were built from these same isolates, so this describes the reference set and is not independent validation.

Theory: the context-dependent grammar

Ciprofloxacin binds through a water and Mg2+ bridge to a serine and an acidic residue in GyrA and ParC. A change at one position matters according to the others around it.

Explore a ciprotype

Schematic reading of the grammar, not a structure calculation. Compare with Figure 6 of the paper.

    Grammar rules: three checkpoints

    Checkpoint 1: GyrA-83, GyrA-87, ParC-80 and ParC-84. The four residues are read together, because a residue can be in both columns of the table (for example D and E). A call needs all four residues in one column: all in the Susceptible column is susceptible, all in the Resistant column is resistant. In any other case checkpoint 1 is inconclusive.

    Checkpoint 2: RecN-346. Leucine encoded by cta is susceptible. Leucine encoded by ctg, or phenylalanine, is resistant. Anything else is not considered.

    Checkpoint 3: qnr genes. qnrB2 and qnrS1 both absent is susceptible. Either or both present is resistant.

    Combined. Resistant if any checkpoint that gives a call says resistant. Susceptible only if all of them say susceptible. When checkpoint 1 is inconclusive, checkpoints 2 and 3 decide.

    A ciprotype that is in the reference isolates takes the phenotype of the majority of those isolates. A new ciprotype is called by the three checkpoints. The reference isolates carry no RecN-346 codon, so checkpoint 2 for ctg could not be scored on them. The hydrophobicity <H> in a result is not part of any checkpoint (how it is calculated).

    Amino acid hydrophobicity (Eisenberg normalised consensus)

    Sequence types

    The sequence type (ST) is read with PubMLST schemes: Achtman for E. coli, S. sonnei and S. enterica, Diancourt for K. pneumoniae. An ST is given only when all seven alleles match exactly and the profile exists; otherwise the nearest alleles are shown. The STs of the reference isolates come from Table S1. Scheme data are from the tseemann/mlst database (downloaded 2026-03-11) and PubMLST; please cite PubMLST (Jolley et al. 2018).

    Limits

    • Four species only: E. coli, S. sonnei, S. enterica subsp. I and K. pneumoniae. Choose the species yourself. A genome that does not match it gets no result. E. coli and S. sonnei cannot be told apart from these genes.
    • The founding dataset is limited. It holds 2,734 isolates from 28 countries, more than half from the USA, and from only three isolation sources (stool, urine and respiratory). More populations need to be studied before the rules can be trusted widely.
    • A qnr gene found in the sequence does not prove it is expressed. Some qnr-positive isolates in the data are susceptible.
    • The accuracy figures are measured on the 2,734 founding isolates that also define the rules, so they are not independent validation.
    • Assembled sequence only. Raw sequencing reads are not supported yet.

    About Ciprotyper
    
  
    
    
    Ciprotyper is an open web server, reference repository and REST API for predicting ciprofloxacin resistance in Escherichia coli, Shigella sonnei, Salmonella enterica subsp. I and Klebsiella pneumoniae. It reads five codon positions in gyrA, parC and recN and the plasmid genes qnrB2 and qnrS1, joins them into a ciprotype, and explains every call with the reference isolates and rules behind it. The Help menu has the rules, the documentation and the API.

    Privacy policy
    Last updated November 2026. ← About
    
  
    
    
    What we do not collect. There are no accounts, no advertising and no tracking or analytics. The site sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser. Sequences you upload for a prediction are deleted after analysis.
    
    
    Predictions. A genome you upload, paste or fetch with a BV-BRC ID is analysed in a temporary folder and deleted when the request ends. The prediction is not stored. For a BV-BRC ID, the ID is sent to BV-BRC to get the assembly. To speed up identical requests, the analysis of an input may be kept briefly in the server's memory.
    
    
    Submissions. When you submit an isolate we store the ciprotype, the residues and the qnr result, the sequence type, the prediction and the details you enter: species, isolation source, genome ID and name, country, year, laboratory phenotype, your name, affiliation, publication link and contact email, the time of your agreement and the version of the consent notice. Each submission gets a submission ID. The genome file is not stored.
    
    
    Who sees what.
    
    
      
      
    • If an isolate is approved, it is public: the isolate data, your submitter name, affiliation, publication link and the submission date are shown in Browse and in the downloads and API.
      • 
      
    • Your email stays private. Only the curators use it, to contact you about your submission.
      • 
      
    • Anyone who has your submission ID can see its status and the prediction, but not your name or email.
      • 
    
    
    
    Stored on the server. Submission records, including the email address, are stored on the server and are available only to the maintainers. When you enter a BV-BRC genome ID, the ID is sent to BV-BRC to get the assembly; nothing else about you is sent.
    
    
    IP addresses and logs. Your IP address is used in the server's memory to limit repeated requests. It is not stored with your submission. The hosting service may keep its own server logs.
    
    
    Keeping and removing data. Submissions are kept until they are removed. To correct or remove your submission, write to ikbalsk.personal@gmail.com with your submission ID. We can remove the record and take an approved isolate out of the repository, the statistics and the predictions. Copies that other people already downloaded cannot be recalled.
    
    
    Changes. We may update this policy and will change the date above.
