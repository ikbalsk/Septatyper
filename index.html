How a call is made

  1. Read the codonsBLAST finds gyrA, parC and recN in your sequence and reads GyrA-83, GyrA-87, ParC-80, ParC-84 and RecN-346 as codons, so synonymous changes are kept.
  2. Find the qnr genesqnrB and qnrS family genes count as present at 80% identity and 90% coverage.
  3. Build the septatypeThe seven values join into one string, for example L-N-I-E-L-0-0.
  4. Call the phenotypeThe majority phenotype among reference isolates with that septatype wins. A new septatype is called by the physicochemical grammar and sent for curation.

Reference performance

Accuracy of the majority rule on the founding isolates. The rules were built from these same isolates, so this describes the reference set and is not independent validation.

Theory: the context-dependent grammar

Ciprofloxacin binds through a water and Mg2+ bridge to a serine and an acidic residue in GyrA and ParC. A change at one position matters according to the others around it.

Explore a septatype

Schematic reading of the grammar, not a structure calculation. Compare with Figure 6 of the paper.

    Grammar rules

    PositionSusceptibleResistant
    GyrA-83Polar residue with -OH groupHydrophobic residue
    GyrA-87Negatively charged residuePolar or uncharged residue
    ParC-80Polar residue with -OH groupHydrophobic residue
    ParC-84Negatively charged residueUncharged or negatively charged residue

    A negatively charged ParC-84 counts in the Resistant column only when ParC-80 is hydrophobic.

    See the worked calculation of the hydrophobicity value and the cutoff

    Two or more votes mean Resistant and none means Susceptible. One vote is settled by the combined mean hydrophobicity <H> of the four residues against 0.0 (Eisenberg scale). Overrides on a susceptible baseline: a qnr gene present gives Resistant, S. sonnei with RecN-346 F gives Resistant, S. enterica F-D-S-E with the ctg codon gives Resistant, and a single GyrA-83 or GyrA-87 change in S. sonnei gives Resistant.

    The grammar is only used for septatypes not seen in the reference isolates. Residue classes marked as assumptions (for example G, P and C counted as hydrophobic) are set in app/grammar.py.

    About SeptaTyper
    
  
    
    
    Method
    
    
    The septatype combines the residues at GyrA-83, GyrA-87, ParC-80, ParC-84 and RecN-346 with the presence or absence of qnrB and qnrS family genes. Residues are read from codons so that synonymous changes, such as the RecN-346 cta and ctg codons in Salmonella, are kept. Genes are located with BLAST+ against reference genes for the chosen species, then aligned to that reference to read each codon. The qnr genes are detected with qnrB2 and qnrS1 reference alleles.
    
    
    Sequence types
    
    
    The sequence type (ST) of an uploaded genome is read with PubMLST schemes: Achtman for E. coli, S. sonnei and S. enterica, Diancourt for K. pneumoniae. An ST is given only when all seven alleles match exactly and the profile exists. Otherwise the nearest alleles are shown. The STs of the reference isolates come from Table S1. Scheme data were taken from the tseemann/mlst database (downloaded 2026-03-11) and the PubMLST website; cite PubMLST (Jolley et al. 2018).
    
    
    What the numbers mean
    
    
    Reference accuracy is calculated on the 2,734 founding isolates that also define the rules. It is not independent validation. The grammar accuracy is also scored on these isolates, with rules written while looking at them.
    
    
    Limits
    
    
      
      
    • Four species only: E. coli, S. sonnei, S. enterica subsp. I and K. pneumoniae. Choose the species yourself. E. coli and S. sonnei cannot be told apart from these genes.
      • 
      
    • A qnr gene found in sequence does not prove it is expressed. Some qnr-positive isolates in the data are susceptible.
      • 
      
    • The result is a prediction. Confirm with laboratory susceptibility testing. Research use only.
      • 
      
    • Raw sequencing reads are not supported yet. Upload assembled sequence.
      • 
    
    
    
    Privacy
    
    
    Uploaded sequence is analysed in a temporary folder and deleted. Submissions keep only the derived genotype, the metadata you enter and the result.
